For the business reader

A weekly macro briefing, produced end-to-end by software.

The dashboard is not a report someone writes each week. It is the output of an eleven-stage pipeline that pulls public data, scores what's moved, drafts commentary, validates against a ten-pass quality gate, and emails the result. The reader sees the briefing; the system below is what makes it trustworthy.

Skip to technical detail ↓
Where data comes from

Five public sources

FRED (Federal Reserve), BLS (labor stats), EIA (energy), the University of Michigan sentiment survey, and earnings-call transcripts from the major banks.

What software does

Score, write, validate

Agents compare each metric to a curated baseline, flag the divergences, draft a 2–4 sentence read of what it means, and refuse to publish if a quality check fails.

What humans do

Curate the rules

The "what counts as normal" baselines, the editorial copy rules, and the visual contract are human-curated. The agents apply them; they don't invent them.

In one sentence

Every published number traces to a public source; every sentence of commentary is gated by a deterministic check before it ships.

Guardrails

AGENT_DISABLE_ALL

Hard kill-switch across every agentic LLM call.

AGENT_MAX_LLM_CALLS

Per-run cost cap across the four LLM agents.

PATH ALLOWLIST

Writable paths per agent; layout/renderer never on it.

AUDIT LOG

Every LLM call → data/agent_memory.jsonl.
