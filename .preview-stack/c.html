Chapter 01

The problem.

A weekly read of the U.S. macro picture used to be a research analyst's full week — pull data, reconcile sources, spot what's moved, decide what it means, write it up, ship it. The result was good but slow, and the human cost was high. The question was whether software could do the assembly and let the human do the judging.

Chapter 02

The approach.

Split the work into eleven small jobs. Each agent has one responsibility, a strict input/output contract, and — for the ones that call Claude — a kill-switch, cost cap, and path allowlist. The structure is deterministic; only the prose is generative.

Chapter 03

A Friday morning, end to end.

06:00 UTC — preflight checks every FRED series and every Claude model ID. 06:05 — collector pulls. 06:20 — analyzer scores against known-normal bands. 06:30 — Claude drafts commentary. 06:35 — renderer patches the page. 06:40 — validator runs ten passes. 06:45 — publisher sends email. Each step has its own log, its own artifact, and its own reason to halt.

Chapter 04

The architecture, briefly.

Deterministic Python in the middle, Claude at the edges. Every agentic call goes through one guardrail file. The renderer never re-runs HTML it didn't write; the validator gates every publish.

Chapter 05

What keeps it honest.

Three contracts and a gate. The visual contract is style_guide.md. The data contract is playbook.md. The editorial contract is §2 of the playbook. The gate is the validator — ten passes, build-blocking, no human override on critical divergences.

Pass 1–3

Internal consistency, source verification, staleness.

Pass 3c

Earnings-quote verbatim gate.

Pass 3i

Cross-source agreement (FRED vs BLS).

Pass 7–8

Visual QA + AI vision review.

Chapter 06

What's still a judgement call.

The system can score divergences against a baseline, but it can't decide which divergence matters most this week. That call still lives with the editor. The agents flag, draft, and validate; a human chooses the lede and stands behind it.
